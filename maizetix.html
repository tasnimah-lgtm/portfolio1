UX/UI DESIGN · TICKETING

MaizeTix

Redesigning the University of Michigan's student ticketing experience to make finding and purchasing tickets clearer, faster, and easier to navigate.

01 / PROJECT OVERVIEW

Designing a clearer way to find student tickets.

MaizeTix is a University of Michigan ticketing platform that allows students to buy and resell tickets for athletic events.

Our project focused on improving the general admissions experience by creating a clearer, more intuitive path from browsing games to finding available tickets.

02 / UNDERSTANDING THE CURRENT EXPERIENCE

Where the existing experience falls short.

Since this was an extension of an existing platform, we worked within MaizeTix's established visual identity rather than creating an entirely new brand.

We analyzed the existing general admissions experience to identify areas where hierarchy, navigation, and ticket discovery could be improved.

CURRENT EXPERIENCE

Low visual hierarchy

Important information and actions compete for attention, making it difficult for users to understand where to look first.

Limited filtering clarity

Filtering options are not visually prominent, making it harder for users to narrow down available tickets.

Stadium interaction

The existing experience does not provide a clear visual way to explore ticket availability by stadium section.

Iconography

Some icons lack clarity and consistency, making certain actions less immediately understandable.

03 / COMPETITIVE ANALYSIS

Learning from existing ticketing platforms.

To better understand expectations for digital ticketing, we analyzed leading platforms including StubHub, Ticketmaster, Vivid Seats, and TickPick.

We compared navigation, information hierarchy, filtering, ticket discovery, and checkout interactions to identify opportunities for improving the MaizeTix experience.

SWOT ANALYSIS

Strengths

Established ticket marketplace and direct connection to U-M students.

Weaknesses

Information hierarchy and ticket discovery could be clearer.

Opportunities

Improve filtering, stadium navigation, and purchasing flow.

Threats

Students can compare MaizeTix with other ticket marketplaces.

04 / DESIGN PROCESS

Designing around clarity and ease of navigation.

We focused on three priorities: clarity, accessibility, and streamlined navigation. We began by defining key user goals and restructuring the experience around how users naturally search for tickets.

We then created wireframes and low-fidelity prototypes to explore different layouts and information hierarchies before refining the experience into higher-fidelity designs based on feedback.

05 / BUILDING THE INTERFACE

Creating a consistent interface.

We created a reusable set of components to keep the redesigned experience consistent across different sports and ticketing flows.

06 / REDESIGNING THE GENERAL ADMISSION EXPERIENCE

A step-by-step path from game selection to ticket availability.

We restructured the experience into a step-by-step flow that progressively introduces information instead of presenting users with every option at once.

Browse games
→
Select tickets
→
Choose quantity
→
Explore availability
→
Purchase
FRAME 01

Browse matches

Users begin by browsing available matches throughout the season. Each event provides the key information needed to decide whether to continue.

FRAME 02

Select ticket quantity

After selecting a match, users choose how many tickets they need. This allows the experience to prioritize ticket options based on their needs.

FRAME 03

Explore ticket availability

Users are presented with available tickets, with recommended options surfaced first for easier comparison. A visual stadium map allows users to explore individual sections and see available tickets.

07 / FINAL EXPERIENCE

One experience, multiple sports.

The redesigned interaction model was designed to work across different athletic events, creating a consistent experience while adapting to the needs of each sport.

08 / REFLECTION

Designing a transactional experience with less friction.

This project taught me how to design a transactional experience where users need to make several decisions before completing a purchase. Rather than overwhelming users with every ticket option at once, we focused on progressively revealing information and giving users clearer control over their choices.
