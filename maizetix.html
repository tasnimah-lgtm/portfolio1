UX/UI DESIGN · TICKETING

MaizeTix

Redesigning the University of Michigan's student ticketing experience to create a clearer path from browsing games to finding available tickets.

01 / PROJECT OVERVIEW

Redesigning a platform built around U-M sports.

MaizeTix is a University of Michigan student ticketing platform that connects students buying and reselling tickets for athletic events. The platform is centered around the student experience of attending U-M sporting events, including football, basketball, hockey, and baseball.

Building on the existing MaizeTix platform, our project focused on redesigning the general admission experience while maintaining the established brand and design patterns.

We explored how the experience could better organize game discovery, ticket information, and availability while translating the existing website experience into a mobile format.

02 / UNDERSTANDING THE CURRENT EXPERIENCE

Identifying opportunities within the existing experience.

Since this was an extension of an existing platform, we worked within MaizeTix's established visual identity rather than creating an entirely new brand.

We analyzed the existing general admission experience to understand how event information, filters, ticket options, and the stadium view were currently presented.

Low visual hierarchy

Important information and actions have similar visual weight, making the interface harder to scan.

Filtering clarity

Filtering options could be made more noticeable and easier to identify within the interface.

Stadium interaction

The existing stadium view provided an opportunity to make section information more visual and easier to explore.

Iconography

Updating iconography could create clearer visual cues for different actions.

03 / COMPETITIVE ANALYSIS

Understanding the ticketing landscape.

We conducted a competitive analysis of established ticketing platforms including Ticketmaster, StubHub, Vivid Seats, and TickPick.

We compared navigation, information hierarchy, filtering, ticket discovery, seating, and purchasing interactions to identify common patterns and opportunities that could inform our own design decisions for MaizeTix.

SWOT ANALYSIS

We synthesized our competitive research into a SWOT analysis to identify strengths, weaknesses, opportunities, and threats within the ticketing experience.

04 / DESIGN PROCESS

Designing around clarity and ease of navigation.

We focused on clarity, accessibility, and streamlined navigation. We began by defining key user goals and restructuring the experience around how users move through the ticketing process.

We then created wireframes and low-fidelity prototypes to explore different layouts and information hierarchies before refining the experience into higher-fidelity designs.

05 / BUILDING THE INTERFACE

Creating a consistent interface.

We created reusable components to keep the redesigned experience consistent across different sports and ticketing flows.

06 / REDESIGNING THE GENERAL ADMISSION EXPERIENCE

A clearer path from game selection to ticket availability.

We restructured the experience into a step-by-step flow that progressively introduces information instead of presenting users with every option at once.

07 / The New User Flow

The redesigned experience

FRAME 01

Browse matches

Users begin by browsing available matches throughout the season. Each event provides key information to help users decide which game they want to explore.

FRAME 02

Select ticket quantity

After selecting a match, users choose how many tickets they would like to purchase before moving forward to the available ticket options.

FRAME 03

Explore ticket availability

Users are presented with available tickets, with recommended options surfaced first for easier comparison. A visual stadium map provides another way to explore available sections.

Recommended tickets

Surfaces available ticket options to make comparison easier.

Stadium view

Provides a visual way to explore different areas of the stadium.

Section selection

Allows users to explore available ticket options by section.

One experience, multiple sports.

The redesigned interaction model was designed to work across different athletic events, creating consistent patterns while adapting the experience to each sport.
